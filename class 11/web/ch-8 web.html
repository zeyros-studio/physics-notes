1. Stress, Strain & Hooke's Law Fundamentals
Longitudinal Stress & Strain
Must Know
$$\text{Tensile / Compressive Stress } (\sigma) = \frac{F}{A}, \quad \text{Longitudinal Strain } (\epsilon) = \frac{\Delta L}{L}$$
Symbols: $F$: Applied normal force ($\text{N}$), $A$: Cross-sectional area ($\text{m}^2$), $L$: Original length, $\Delta L$: Elongation/compression.
SI Units: Stress: $\text{N m}^{-2}$ or Pascal ($\text{Pa}$); Strain: Dimensionless [$M^0L^0T^0$].
Conditions: Force $F$ must act perpendicular (normal) to the cross-sectional area $A$.
Shearing & Hydraulic (Volume) Strain
Must Know
$$\text{Shearing Strain } (\theta) = \frac{\Delta x}{L} = \tan \theta \approx \theta, \quad \text{Volume Strain } = \frac{\Delta V}{V}$$
Symbols: $\Delta x$: Lateral displacement of top face, $L$: Distance between opposite fixed faces, $\Delta V$: Change in volume, $V$: Original volume.
Shear Stress: $\sigma_s = \frac{F_{\parallel}}{A}$, where $F_{\parallel}$ is tangential force applied parallel to the surface.
Hooke's Law
Must Know Exam Favourite
$$\text{Stress} \propto \text{Strain} \implies \text{Stress} = k \times \text{Strain} \implies k = \frac{\text{Stress}}{\text{Strain}}$$
Modulus ($k$): Proportionality constant known as **Modulus of Elasticity**. Depends on material nature and temperature, independent of dimensions.
Validity: Strictly valid only within the proportional limit / small deformations ($\epsilon < 1\%$).

⚠ EXAM CAUTION & INTERPRETATION OF STRESS-STRAIN GRAPHS:
1. Ductile vs. Brittle Materials: If fracture point $E$ is far from ultimate strength $D$, material is Ductile. If $D$ and $E$ are close, it is Brittle.
2. Young's Modulus from Slope: $Y = \text{Slope of linear region } (OA) = \frac{\Delta \sigma}{\Delta \epsilon}$. Higher slope $\implies$ More rigid / elastic material (e.g., $Y_{\text{steel}} > Y_{\text{copper}}$).
3. Three Elastic Moduli (Young's, Shear, Bulk) Core Formulations
1. Young's Modulus ($Y$) — Length Change
Must Know Exam Favourite
$$Y = \frac{\text{Tensile or Compressive Stress}}{\text{Longitudinal Strain}} = \frac{F / A}{\Delta L / L} = \frac{F \cdot L}{A \cdot \Delta L}$$
For Circular Wire: $A = \pi r^2 = \frac{\pi d^2}{4} \implies Y = \frac{4 F L}{\pi d^2 \Delta L} \implies \Delta L = \frac{4 F L}{\pi d^2 Y}$
Key Property: Defined ONLY FOR SOLIDS. Steel is more elastic than copper/rubber because it requires greater force for the same strain.
2. Shear Modulus ($G$ or $\eta$) — Shape Change
Must Know
$$G = \frac{\text{Shearing Stress}}{\text{Shearing Strain}} = \frac{F_{\parallel} / A}{\Delta x / L} = \frac{F_{\parallel} / A}{\theta} = \frac{F_{\parallel}}{A \cdot \theta}$$
Empirical Relation: For most solids, Shear Modulus is approximately one-third of Young's Modulus: $G \approx \frac{Y}{3}$.
Applicability: Defined ONLY FOR SOLIDS (Fluids cannot sustain static shearing stress).
3. Bulk Modulus ($B$) & Compressibility ($k$) — Volume Change
Must Know Exam Favourite
$$B = -\frac{p}{\Delta V / V} = -V \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta V}, \quad \text{Compressibility } k = \frac{1}{B} = -\frac{1}{V} \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta p}$$
Negative Sign: Indicates that increase in pressure ($p > 0$) causes decrease in volume ($\Delta V < 0$), keeping $B > 0$.
Fluid Depth Form: Pressure at depth $h$: $p = h \rho g \implies \frac{\Delta V}{V} = \frac{h \rho g}{B}$.
States Comparison: $B_{\text{solid}} > B_{\text{liquid}} > B_{\text{gas}}$. Gases are $\sim 10^6$ times more compressible than solids.
Modulus Stress Type Strain Type Formula Applicable State
Young's ($Y$) Normal ($F/A$) Longitudinal ($\Delta L/L$) $Y = \frac{F L}{A \Delta L}$ Solids only
Shear ($G$) Tangential ($F_{\parallel}/A$) Shear angle ($\theta = \Delta x / L$) $G = \frac{F_{\parallel}}{A \theta}$ Solids only
Bulk ($B$) Hydraulic ($p$) Volume ($\Delta V/V$) $B = -\frac{p V}{\Delta V}$ Solids, Liquids, Gases
4. Poisson's Ratio & Elastic Potential Energy Derived Properties
Poisson's Ratio ($\nu$ or $\sigma$)
Must Know Special Case
$$\nu = -\frac{\text{Lateral Strain}}{\text{Longitudinal Strain}} = -\frac{\Delta d / d}{\Delta L / L} = -\frac{\Delta r / r}{\Delta L / L}$$
Unit & Dimension: Pure numerical ratio. Dimensionless [$M^0L^0T^0$].
Theoretical Limit: $-1 \leq \nu \leq 0.5$. Practical value for engineering metals: $0.28 \leq \nu \leq 0.33$.
Derivation: Strain Energy / Elastic Potential Energy in Stretched Wire
1. Restoring force at elongation $x$: $F(x) = \frac{Y A x}{L}$
2. Work done for infinitesimal elongation $dx$: $dW = F(x) \, dx = \frac{Y A x}{L} \, dx$
3. Total work done for total extension $l$: $$W = \int_{0}^{l} \frac{Y A x}{L} \, dx = \frac{Y A}{L} \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_0^l = \frac{1}{2} \frac{Y A l^2}{L} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{Y A l}{L} \right) l = \boxed{\frac{1}{2} F \cdot l}$$
4. Energy per unit volume (Energy Density $u$): $$u = \frac{U}{\text{Volume}} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} F \cdot l}{A \cdot L} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{F}{A}\right) \left(\frac{l}{L}\right) = \boxed{\frac{1}{2} \times \text{Stress} \times \text{Strain} = \frac{1}{2} Y (\text{Strain})^2}$$
5. Engineering Applications & Real-World Methods Exam Oriented
Bending of Beams & Maximum Mountain Height
Exam Favourite Shortcut
$$\text{Sag / Deflection } (\delta) = \frac{W L^3}{4 b d^3 Y}, \quad h_{\text{max}} \approx \frac{\sigma_{\text{elastic}}}{\rho g} \approx 10 \text{ km}$$
Beam Optimization: To minimize bending $\delta$, depth $d$ is increased rather than width $b$ because $\delta \propto d^{-3}$. I-section beams prevent buckling and save material.
Crane Steel Rope: Minimum area required to lift load $M$: $A \ge \frac{M g}{\sigma_y}$. Uses braided thin wires for flexibility and strength.
6. Standard Numerical Solving Algorithms Step-by-Step Methods
METHOD 1: Two Wires Connected End-to-End (Series Load)
Identify Same Tension: $F_1 = F_2 = F = W$ → Express Elongations: $\Delta L_1 = \frac{F L_1}{A_1 Y_1}, \, \Delta L_2 = \frac{F L_2}{A_2 Y_2}$ → Ratio Formula: $\frac{\Delta L_1}{\Delta L_2} = \left(\frac{Y_2}{Y_1}\right) \left(\frac{L_1}{L_2}\right) \left(\frac{A_2}{A_1}\right)$ → Total Elongation: $\Delta L_{\text{total}} = \Delta L_1 + \Delta L_2$
METHOD 2: Wire Whirled in a Vertical Circle (Bottom Point)
Max Tension at Lowest Point: $T_{\text{max}} = M g + M \omega^2 L$ → Set Stress Equation: $\sigma_{\text{max}} = \frac{T_{\text{max}}}{A}$ → Calculate Elongation: $\Delta L = \frac{T_{\text{max}} L}{A Y}$
METHOD 3: Density Variation under High Hydraulic Pressure
Mass Conservation: $m = \rho V = \rho' V'$ → Volume Change: $V' = V(1 - \frac{p}{B})$ → New Density: $\rho' = \frac{\rho}{1 - \frac{p}{B}} \approx \rho \left(1 + \frac{p}{B}\right)$
7. Common Calculation Mistakes & Exam Checklist Error Prevention
⛔ COMMON STUDENT ERRORS & MISTAKES:
1. Diameter vs. Radius Confusion: $A = \pi r^2 = \pi \left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{\pi d^2}{4}$. Forgetting to halve diameter is the #1 mistake.
2. Unit Conversions: $1 \text{ GPa} = 10^9 \text{ N m}^{-2}$, $1 \text{ MPa} = 10^6 \text{ N m}^{-2}$, $1 \text{ atm} = 1.013 \times 10^5 \text{ Pa}$, $1 \text{ cm}^2 = 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2$, $1 \text{ mm}^2 = 10^{-6} \text{ m}^2$.
3. Tension in Mid-wire: In a hanging wire loaded with weight $W$, tension at any section is $W$, NOT $2W$.
4. Elasticity Misconception: Steel is more elastic than rubber because steel requires a much larger stress for the same strain.
