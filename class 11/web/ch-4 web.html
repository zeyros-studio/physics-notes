1. Momentum & Newton's Second Law

Linear Momentum & Newton's Second Law Fundamental
$$\vec{p} = m\vec{v}, \quad \vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = m\vec{a}$$
Symbols: $\vec{p}$ = momentum, $m$ = mass, $\vec{v}$ = velocity SI Unit: $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$ or $\text{N}\cdot\text{s}$[cite: 31] Component Form: $F_x = m a_x, \; F_y = m a_y, \; F_z = m a_z$[cite: 19] Key Rule: Force at an instant determines acceleration at that exact instant[cite: 19].
Impulse-Momentum Theorem Shortcut
$$\vec{J} = \int \vec{F} \, dt = \Delta \vec{p} = \vec{p}_{\text{final}} - \vec{p}_{\text{initial}}$$
Application: Impulsive forces acting for very short durations[cite: 20]. Rebound Wall Collision: Normal impulse for speed $u$ at angle $\theta$ is $J = 2mu\cos\theta$[cite: 21].

2. Newton's Third Law & Conservation of Momentum

Action-Reaction & Conservation Principle Must Know
$$\vec{F}_{AB} = -\vec{F}_{BA}, \quad \sum \vec{p}_{\text{initial}} = \sum \vec{p}_{\text{final}} \text{ (for isolated system)}$$
Crucial Note: Action and reaction act on different bodies; they never cancel each other out[cite: 21]. Gun Recoil: $m_s v_s + m_g v_g = 0 \implies v_g = -\frac{m_s v_s}{m_g}$[cite: 21]

3. Equilibrium of a Particle

Concurrent Forces in Equilibrium Exam Favourite
$$\sum \vec{F} = 0 \implies \sum F_x = 0, \; \sum F_y = 0, \; \sum F_z = 0$$
Lami's Theorem (3 Forces): $\frac{F_1}{\sin\alpha} = \frac{F_2}{\sin\beta} = \frac{F_3}{\sin\gamma}$ Triangle Law: Three forces in equilibrium form a closed triangle with cyclic arrows[cite: 22].

4. Friction Laws & Mechanics

Static & Kinetic Friction
$$f_s \le \mu_s N, \quad f_k = \mu_k N$$
Angle of Repose ($\theta$): $\mu_s = \tan\theta_{\text{max}}$[cite: 25] Note: $\mu_k < \mu_s$. Static friction is self-adjusting[cite: 26, 32].
Elevator / Lift Apparent Weight
$$R = m(g \pm a)$$
Upward Accel ($+a$): $R = m(g+a)$ (Feels heavier) Downward Accel ($-a$): $R = m(g-a)$ (Feels lighter)

5. Circular Motion & Banking of Roads

Vehicle on Banked Road with Friction Exam Favourite
$$v_{\text{max}} = \sqrt{Rg \left(\frac{\mu_s + \tan\theta}{1 - \mu_s \tan\theta}\right)}, \quad v_0 = \sqrt{Rg \tan\theta} \text{ (Optimum Speed)}$$
Level Road Max Speed: $v_{\text{max}} = \sqrt{\mu_s Rg}$[cite: 27] Centripetal Force: $F_c = \frac{mv^2}{R}$ directed towards the center[cite: 27].

6. Systematic Problem-Solving Method (FBD Technique)

Standard 5-Step FBD Solution Framework:
  1. System Isolation: Choose a convenient part of the assembly as your system[cite: 29].
  2. Free-Body Diagram (FBD): Isolate the system and draw all external contact and field forces acting on it[cite: 30].
  3. Coordinate Axes: Set orthogonal axes (e.g., parallel and perpendicular to an incline).
  4. Apply Equations of Motion: Use $\sum F_x = m a_x$ and $\sum F_y = m a_y$ (or $\sum F = 0$ for equilibrium).
  5. Newton's Third Law pairing: Link internal forces across connected bodies (e.g., strings $T$, normal contacts $N$)[cite: 30].
