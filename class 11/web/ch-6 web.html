1. Centre of Mass (CM)

Topic: Position Vector of Centre of Mass
\[\vec{R}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i \vec{r}_i}{M} = \frac{\int \vec{r} \, dm}{M}\]
Discrete Components: \(X_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum m_i x_i}{M}, \quad Y_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum m_i y_i}{M}, \quad Z_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum m_i z_i}{M}\)
Continuous Components: \(X_{\text{cm}} = \frac{1}{M} \int x \, dm, \quad Y_{\text{cm}} = \frac{1}{M} \int y \, dm, \quad Z_{\text{cm}} = \frac{1}{M} \int z \, dm\)
Topic: Kinematics & Dynamics of Centre of Mass
\[\vec{V}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{d\vec{R}_{\text{cm}}}{dt} = \frac{\sum m_i \vec{v}_i}{M} = \frac{\vec{P}_{\text{total}}}{M}\] \[\vec{A}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{d\vec{V}_{\text{cm}}}{dt} = \frac{\sum m_i \vec{a}_i}{M} = \frac{\vec{F}_{\text{ext}}}{M}\]
Conservation of Momentum: If \(\vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = 0 \implies \vec{P}_{\text{total}} = \text{constant}\) and \(\vec{V}_{\text{cm}} = \text{constant}\).
Shortcut: Mass Removal / Cut-out Bodies
When a shape of mass \(m'\) is removed from a uniform object of mass \(M_0\), the new centre of mass relative to original origin is: \[X_{\text{new}} = \frac{M_0 X_0 - m' x'}{M_0 - m'}\]

2. Vector Product & Kinematics of Rotation

Topic: Vector (Cross) Product
\[\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = (AB \sin\theta) \hat{n} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ A_x & A_y & A_z \\ B_x & B_y & B_z \end{vmatrix}\]
Properties: Anti-commutative (\(\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = -\vec{B} \times \vec{A}\)). Unit vectors: \(\hat{i}\times\hat{j}=\hat{k}, \, \hat{j}\times\hat{k}=\hat{i}, \, \hat{k}\times\hat{i}=\hat{j}\).
Topic: Relation Between Linear and Angular Variables
\[\vec{v} = \vec{\omega} \times \vec{r} \implies v = \omega r_\perp\] \[\vec{a}_t = \vec{\alpha} \times \vec{r} \implies a_t = \alpha r_\perp \quad \text{and} \quad a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r\]
Angular Kinematic Equations (Constant \(\alpha\)): \[\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t \quad | \quad \theta = \theta_0 + \omega_0 t + \frac{1}{2}\alpha t^2 \quad | \quad \omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha(\theta - \theta_0)\]

3. Torque & Angular Momentum

Topic: Torque (Moment of Force)
\[\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F} \implies \tau = r F \sin\theta = r_\perp F = r F_\perp\]
SI Unit: \(\text{N m}\) | Dimensions: \([ML^2T^{-2}]\)
Rotational Form: \(\vec{\tau}_{\text{ext}} = I \vec{\alpha}\) (for rotation about a fixed axis).
Topic: Angular Momentum (Moment of Linear Momentum)
\[\vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p} \implies L = r p \sin\theta = r_\perp p\] \[\vec{L}_z = I \vec{\omega} \quad \text{(for symmetric rigid bodies rotating about fixed z-axis)}\]
SI Unit: \(\text{J s}\) or \(\text{kg m}^2 \text{s}^{-1}\) | Dimensions: \([ML^2T^{-1}]\)
Fundamental Dynamic Law: \(\vec{\tau}_{\text{ext}} = \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt}\)
Must Know: Law of Conservation of Angular Momentum
If the net external torque acting on a system is zero (\(\vec{\tau}_{\text{ext}} = 0\)): \[\frac{d\vec{L}}{dt} = 0 \implies \vec{L} = \text{constant} \implies I_1 \omega_1 = I_2 \omega_2\] Application: A spinning acrobat folding arms reduces \(I\), causing angular speed \(\omega\) to increase.

4. Moment of Inertia (\(I\)) & Energy

Topic: Moment of Inertia & Radius of Gyration
\[I = \sum_{i=1}^n m_i r_i^2 = \int r^2 \, dm \quad \text{and} \quad I = M k^2 \implies k = \sqrt{\frac{I}{M}}\]
SI Unit: \(\text{kg m}^2\) | Dimensions: \([ML^2]\)
Rotational Kinetic Energy: \(K_{\text{rot}} = \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2 = \frac{L^2}{2I}\)
Work & Power: \(W = \int \tau \, d\theta, \quad P = \vec{\tau} \cdot \vec{\omega}\)

6. Equilibrium & Levers

Topic: Conditions for Mechanical Equilibrium
\[\text{Translational Equilibrium: } \vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \sum \vec{F}_i = 0 \implies \vec{P} = \text{constant}\] \[\text{Rotational Equilibrium: } \vec{\tau}_{\text{net}} = \sum \vec{\tau}_i = 0 \implies \vec{L} = \text{constant}\]
Topic: Principle of Moments (Lever Rule)
\[F_1 d_1 = F_2 d_2 \implies \text{Load} \times \text{Load Arm} = \text{Effort} \times \text{Effort Arm}\] \[\text{Mechanical Advantage (M.A.)} = \frac{F_1}{F_2} = \frac{d_2}{d_1}\]

8. Numerical Problem-Solving Methods

Derivation Method: Deriving Rotational Kinematic Equation \(\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\theta\)

By definition of angular acceleration: \(\alpha = \frac{d\omega}{dt} = \frac{d\omega}{d\theta} \cdot \frac{d\theta}{dt} = \omega \frac{d\omega}{d\theta}\)

Rearranging variables for integration:

\[\omega \, d\omega = \alpha \, d\theta\] \[\int_{\omega_0}^{\omega} \omega \, d\omega = \alpha \int_{0}^{\theta} d\theta\] \[\left[ \frac{\omega^2}{2} \right]_{\omega_0}^{\omega} = \alpha \theta \implies \frac{\omega^2 - \omega_0^2}{2} = \alpha \theta\] \[\boxed{\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\theta}\]
Numerical Solver: Flywheel Torque & Work Done Step-by-Step
\(\text{Given Values} \rightarrow \text{Select Equation} \rightarrow \text{Substitute} \rightarrow \text{Calculate} \rightarrow \text{Final Answer}\)

Problem Example: A solid disc flywheel of mass \(M = 20\text{ kg}\) and radius \(R = 0.2\text{ m}\) is pulled with a tangentially applied force \(F = 25\text{ N}\). Find angular acceleration \(\alpha\) and kinetic energy gained after unwinding \(s = 2\text{ m}\) of string.

Step 1: Moment of Inertia of Disc: \(I = \frac{1}{2} M R^2 = \frac{1}{2} (20)(0.2)^2 = 0.4\text{ kg m}^2\)

Step 2: Applied Torque: \(\tau = F \times R = 25 \times 0.2 = 5\text{ N m}\)

Step 3: Angular Acceleration: \(\alpha = \frac{\tau}{I} = \frac{5}{0.4} = \boxed{12.5\text{ rad s}^{-2}}\)

Step 4: Angular Displacement: \(\theta = \frac{s}{R} = \frac{2}{0.2} = 10\text{ rad}\)

Step 5: Work-Energy Theorem Verification: \(W = \tau \theta = 5 \times 10 = \boxed{50\text{ J}}\) (Equal to \(K_{\text{rot}} = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2\)).
