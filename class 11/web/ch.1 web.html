Units and Measurement Section 1: Fundamental Units & Figures

1. Fundamental Units & Angles

Plane Angle & Solid Angle MUST KNOW
$$d\theta = \frac{ds}{r} \text{ (rad)}, \quad d\Omega = \frac{dA}{r^2} \text{ (sr)}$$
Symbols: $ds$: Arc length, $r$: Radius, $dA$: Spherical surface area.
SI Units: Radian ($\text{rad}$) for plane angle; Steradian ($\text{sr}$) for solid angle.
Conditions: Both quantities are geometrically dimensionless ($[M^0L^0T^0]$).
(a) Plane Angle $d\theta = \frac{ds}{r}$
(b) Solid Angle $d\Omega = \frac{dA}{r^2}$

Significant Figures Rules

Rule Condition Example Significant Digits
All non-zero digits are significant. $2308 \text{ cm}$ 4 ($2, 3, 0, 8$)
Zeros between two non-zero digits are significant. $2.008 \text{ m}$ 4 ($2, 0, 0, 8$)
Leading zeros (left of first non-zero digit) are NOT significant. $0.0023 \text{ kg}$ 2 ($2, 3$)
Trailing zeros in a number WITHOUT a decimal point are NOT significant. $12300 \text{ m}$ 3 ($1, 2, 3$)
Trailing zeros in a number WITH a decimal point ARE significant. $3.500 \text{ g}$ 4 ($3, 5, 0, 0$)
⚠️ CAUTION: Unit Conversion Trap
Changing units does NOT alter the number of significant figures. Always express numbers in scientific notation ($a \times 10^b$) to avoid ambiguity.
Example: $4.700 \text{ m} = 4.700 \times 10^2 \text{ cm} = 4.700 \times 10^{-3} \text{ km}$ (All retain 4 significant figures).

Units and Measurement Section 2: Error Analysis & Propagation

2. Propagation of Errors

Error Combination Rules EXAM FAVOURITE
$$\text{Addition/Subtraction: } Z = A \pm B \implies \Delta Z = \Delta A + \Delta B$$ $$\text{Product/Division: } Z = A \cdot B \text{ or } \frac{A}{B} \implies \frac{\Delta Z}{Z} = \frac{\Delta A}{A} + \frac{\Delta B}{B}$$ $$\text{Power Relation: } Z = A^p B^q / C^r \implies \frac{\Delta Z}{Z} = p\frac{\Delta A}{A} + q\frac{\Delta B}{B} + r\frac{\Delta C}{C}$$
Absolute Error: $\Delta Z$ is always additive to compute maximum possible error.
Percentage Error: $\% \text{ Error in } Z = \left(\frac{\Delta Z}{Z}\right) \times 100\%$.

Systematic Numerical-Solving Framework

Methodology: Calculating Error in Derived Physical Quantities
1 State Given Measurements: $Length \ (l) = 16.2 \pm 0.1 \text{ cm}$, $Breadth \ (b) = 10.1 \pm 0.1 \text{ cm}$.
2 Compute Measured Area: $A = l \times b = 16.2 \times 10.1 = 163.62 \text{ cm}^2$.
3 Apply Fractional Error Rule: $\frac{\Delta A}{A} = \frac{\Delta l}{l} + \frac{\Delta b}{b} = \frac{0.1}{16.2} + \frac{0.1}{10.1} \approx 0.00617 + 0.00990 = 0.01607$.
4 Calculate Absolute Uncertainty: $\Delta A = A \times 0.01607 = 163.62 \times 0.01607 = 2.63 \text{ cm}^2 \approx 3 \text{ cm}^2$.
5 Final Reported Result: $\mathbf{A = (164 \pm 3) \text{ cm}^2}$.

Units and Measurement Section 3: Applications of Dimensional Analysis

3. Dimensional Analysis & Applications

Principle of Homogeneity of Dimensions MUST KNOW
$$\text{If } A + B = C \implies [A] = [B] = [C]$$
Rule: Physical quantities can only be added or subtracted if they possess the same dimensions.
Note: Arguments of exponential, trigonometric, and logarithmic functions must be dimensionless.

Rigorous Derivation Method

Derivation of Simple Pendulum Time Period ($T$) via Dimensional Analysis
1 Assume Product Dependence: $T = k \cdot l^x \cdot g^y \cdot m^z$, where $k$ is a dimensionless constant.
2 Substitute Dimensional Formulas: $[M^0 L^0 T^1] = [M^0 L^1 T^0]^x \cdot [M^0 L^1 T^{-2}]^y \cdot [M^1 L^0 T^0]^z$.
3 Collect Exponents: $[M^0 L^0 T^1] = M^z \cdot L^{x+y} \cdot T^{-2y}$.
4 Equate Exponents on Both Sides:
• Mass exponent: $z = 0$
• Time exponent: $-2y = 1 \implies y = -\frac{1}{2}$
• Length exponent: $x + y = 0 \implies x = -y = \frac{1}{2}$
5 Substitute Exponents Back: $T = k \cdot l^{1/2} \cdot g^{-1/2} \cdot m^0 \implies \mathbf{T = k \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}}$.
6 Final Formula (Experimentally $k = 2\pi$): $\mathbf{\boxed{T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}}}$.
⚠️ Limitations of Dimensional Analysis
1. Cannot determine dimensionless constants ($k$, $2\pi$, $\sin\theta$).
2. Fails for expressions containing sum/difference of terms (e.g., $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$).
3. Cannot derive formulas with more than 3 independent physical variables.
