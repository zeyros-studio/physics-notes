1. Scalar Product of Vectors

The scalar (dot) product of two vectors \(\vec{A}\) and \(\vec{B}\) yields a scalar quantity[cite: 52].

Topic: Scalar Product Definition
\[\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = A B \cos\theta\]
Symbols: \(\theta\) = angle between vectors, \(A, B\) = magnitudes.
Component Form: \(\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z\)[cite: 52]
Condition for Perpendicularity: \(\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = 0\) (since \(\cos 90^\circ = 0\))[cite: 52]
Must Know
Unit vector dot products: \(\hat{i}\cdot\hat{i} = \hat{j}\cdot\hat{j} = \hat{k}\cdot\hat{k} = 1\) and \(\hat{i}\cdot\hat{j} = \hat{j}\cdot\hat{k} = \hat{k}\cdot\hat{i} = 0\)[cite: 52].

2. Work & The Work-Energy Theorem

Topic: Work Done by Constant Force
\[W = (F \cos\theta)d = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d}\]
Symbols: \(F\) = force magnitude, \(d\) = displacement, \(\theta\) = angle between force and displacement.
SI Unit: Joule (\(\text{J}\)) or \(\text{kg m}^2 \text{s}^{-2}\). Dimensions: \([ML^2T^{-2}]\)[cite: 54].
Topic: Work Done by Variable Force (1D)
\[W = \int_{x_i}^{x_f} F(x) dx = \text{Area under } F\text{-}x \text{ curve}\]
Topic: Work-Energy Theorem
\[W_{\text{net}} = K_f - K_i = \Delta K\]
Statement: The work done by the net force on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy[cite: 53].
Kinetic Energy: \(K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2\)[cite: 54]
Caution
Work done by friction or viscous drag is generally negative (\(\theta = 180^\circ\)), reducing the kinetic energy of the system[cite: 54].

3. Potential Energy & Conservative Forces

Topic: Conservative Force & Potential Energy relation
\[F(x) = -\frac{dV(x)}{dx} \implies \Delta V = -W_c = -\int_{x_i}^{x_f} F(x) dx\]
Topic: Gravitational & Spring Potential Energy
\[V_{\text{grav}} = mgh \quad \text{and} \quad V_{\text{spring}} = \frac{1}{2}kx^2\]
Hooke's Law (Spring Force): \(F_s = -kx\) (where \(k\) is spring constant in \(\text{N m}^{-1}\)).

4. Conservation of Mechanical Energy

Topic: Total Mechanical Energy (\(E\))
\[E = K + V = \text{constant} \quad (\text{when only conservative forces act})\]
Non-Conservative Forces Present: \(E_f - E_i = W_{\text{nc}}\) (where \(W_{\text{nc}}\) is work done by friction/external non-conservative forces).

5. Power

Topic: Instantaneous & Average Power
\[P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v} \quad \text{and} \quad P_{\text{av}} = \frac{W}{t}\]
SI Unit: Watt (\(\text{W}\)) or \(\text{J s}^{-1}\). Dimensions: \([ML^2T^{-3}]\).
Conversion: \(1\text{ hp} = 746\text{ W}\).

6. Collisions

Topic: One-Dimensional Elastic Collision (Head-on)
\[v_{1f} = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_{1i} \quad \text{and} \quad v_{2f} = \frac{2m_1 v_{1i}}{m_1 + m_2}\]
Key Principle: Total linear momentum is conserved in all collisions. Kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions.
Equal Masses (\(m_1 = m_2\)): \(v_{1f} = 0, \ v_{2f} = v_{1i}\) (Velocities are exchanged).
Exam Favourite
For a 2D elastic collision between two equal masses (\(m_1 = m_2\)) with one stationary target, the two bodies move at right angles to each other after collision (\(\theta_1 + \theta_2 = 90^\circ\)).
