Chapter 2: Motion in a Straight Line

FORMULA & METHOD SHEET

1. Fundamental Kinematic Parameters

Instantaneous Velocity & Acceleration
MUST KNOW
$$v = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{dx}{dt}, \quad a = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{dv}{dt} = v \frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$$
Symbols: $x$ = position, $v$ = instantaneous velocity, $a$ = acceleration, $t$ = time.
SI Units: $v \to \text{m s}^{-1}$, $a \to \text{m s}^{-2}$
Conditions: $a = v \frac{dv}{dx}$ is specifically used when acceleration is expressed as a function of position $x$ rather than time $t$.
CAUTION: Zero Velocity vs. Zero Acceleration
$v = 0$ at an instant does NOT mean $a = 0$. Example: At the apex of vertical throw, $v = 0$ but $a = -g = -9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$.

2. Uniformly Accelerated Rectilinear Motion ($a = \text{constant}$)

Standard Equations of Motion
MUST KNOW EXAM FAVOURITE
$$v = v_0 + at, \quad x = x_0 + v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}a t^2, \quad v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)$$
Symbols: $v_0$ = initial velocity, $v$ = final velocity, $x_0$ = initial position, $x$ = final position.
Average Velocity: $\bar{v} = \frac{v + v_0}{2}$ (Valid only for constant $a$).
Displacement in $n^{\text{th}}$ second: $D_n = v_0 + \frac{a}{2}(2n - 1)$ $\quad [\text{Unit: m}]$
Calculus Derivation of Motion Equations ($a = \text{constant}$)
  1. Derivation of $v = v_0 + at$:
    Start with definition $a = \frac{dv}{dt} \implies dv = a \, dt$. Integrate both sides with limits $(v_0 \to v)$ and $(0 \to t)$: $$\int_{v_0}^{v} dv = a \int_{0}^{t} dt \implies [v]_{v_0}^{v} = a [t]_0^t \implies \boxed{v = v_0 + at}$$
  2. Derivation of $x - x_0 = v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}at^2$:
    Substitute $v = \frac{dx}{dt} \implies dx = v \, dt = (v_0 + at) \, dt$. Integrate both sides with limits $(x_0 \to x)$ and $(0 \to t)$: $$\int_{x_0}^{x} dx = \int_{0}^{t} (v_0 + at) \, dt \implies x - x_0 = v_0 [t]_0^t + a \left[ \frac{t^2}{2} \right]_0^t \implies \boxed{x = x_0 + v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}at^2}$$
  3. Derivation of $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)$:
    Use chain rule $a = v \frac{dv}{dx} \implies v \, dv = a \, dx$. Integrate both sides with limits $(v_0 \to v)$ and $(x_0 \to x)$: $$\int_{v_0}^{v} v \, dv = a \int_{x_0}^{x} dx \implies \left[ \frac{v^2}{2} \right]_{v_0}^{v} = a [x]_{x_0}^{x} \implies \frac{v^2 - v_0^2}{2} = a(x - x_0) \implies \boxed{v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)}$$

3. Vertical Motion Under Gravity (Free Fall)

Free Fall Equations (Sign Convention: Upward = $+y$)
MUST KNOW
$$a = -g \implies v = v_0 - gt, \quad y = y_0 + v_0 t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2, \quad v^2 = v_0^2 - 2g(y - y_0)$$
Max Height ($v=0$): $H_{\text{max}} = \frac{v_0^2}{2g}$
Time to Top: $t_{\text{up}} = \frac{v_0}{g}$
Total Time of Flight: $T = \frac{2v_0}{g}$
Impact Velocity: $v_{\text{impact}} = -v_0$
SHORTCUT: Galileo's Law of Odd Numbers
For a body dropped from rest ($v_0 = 0$) under gravity, distances traversed in equal successive time intervals $\tau$ follow the odd number ratio: $$y_1 : y_2 : y_3 : y_4 : \dots = 1 : 3 : 5 : 7 : \dots \quad \text{where } y_1 = \frac{1}{2}g\tau^2$$

METHODS & SPECIAL CASES

4. Graphical Analysis & Physical Meanings

Position-Time Graph ($x-t$)
• Slope of tangent: Instantaneous velocity ($v = \frac{dx}{dt}$)
• Curvature upward: Positive acceleration ($a > 0$)
Velocity-Time Graph ($v-t$)
• Slope of line: Acceleration ($a = \frac{dv}{dt}$)
• Area under curve: Net Displacement ($\Delta x = \int v \, dt$)

5. Numerical Solving Templates

1. Stopping Distance & Reaction Time Problems
Given: $v_0, a = -d$ → Formula: $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2ax$ ($v=0$) → Stopping Distance: $d_s = \frac{v_0^2}{2a}$
• Reaction Time ($t_r$): Distance traveled during thinking phase before applying brakes = $d_{\text{think}} = v_0 \cdot t_r$
• Total Stopping Distance: $d_{\text{total}} = (v_0 \cdot t_r) + \frac{v_0^2}{2a}$
2. Ball Thrown Up from Building Height $h$ (Single-Step Method)
Given: $y_0 = +h, y = 0, v_0 = +u, a = -g$ → Substitute into: $y = y_0 + v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ → Solve: $0 = h + ut - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$
Worked Numerical Example (NCERT Ex 2.3):
Given: $y_0 = 25\text{ m}, v_0 = +20\text{ m s}^{-1}, a = -10\text{ m s}^{-2}, y = 0$ (ground).
$$0 = 25 + 20t - 5t^2 \implies 5t^2 - 20t - 25 = 0 \implies t^2 - 4t - 5 = 0 \implies (t - 5)(t + 1) = 0$$ $$\text{Discard negative time } t = -1\text{s} \implies \boxed{t = 5\text{ s}}$$

6. Relative Velocity in One Dimension

Relative Velocity Equations
MUST KNOW EXAM FAVOURITE
$$v_{AB} = v_A - v_B, \quad v_{BA} = v_B - v_A = -v_{AB}$$
Same Direction: $v_{\text{rel}} = |v_A - v_B|$
Opposite Direction: $v_{\text{rel}} = v_A + v_B$
Relative Acceleration: $a_{AB} = a_A - a_B \implies x_{\text{rel}} = (v_{AB}) t + \frac{1}{2} (a_{AB}) t^2$
SHORTCUT: Police Van & Thief Bullet Catch Problem (NCERT Ex 2.14)
Formula: $v_{\text{muzzle}}$ is bullet speed relative to police van ($v_P$).
• Absolute speed of bullet: $v_b = v_{\text{muzzle}} + v_P$
• Speed with which bullet hits thief's car ($v_T$): $v_{\text{rel}} = v_b - v_T = (v_{\text{muzzle}} + v_P) - v_T$
