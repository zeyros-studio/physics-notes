1. Temperature Scales & Gas Laws Core Basics
Temperature Scale Conversion Relation EXAM FAVOURITE
$$\frac{t_F - 32}{180} = \frac{t_C}{100} = \frac{T - 273.15}{100}$$
Symbols:
  • $t_C$: Temperature in Celsius ($^\circ\text{C}$)
  • $t_F$: Temperature in Fahrenheit ($^\circ\text{F}$)
  • $T$: Absolute Temperature in Kelvin ($\text{K}$)
Key Constants & Relations:
  • $1$ Division on $t_C$ scale = $1.8$ Divisions on $t_F$ scale
  • Absolute Zero: $0\text{ K} = -273.15^\circ\text{C} = -459.67^\circ\text{F}$
  • Triple Point of Water: $T_{TP} = 273.16\text{ K} = 0.01^\circ\text{C}$
Ideal Gas Equation & Vol. Expansivity
$$PV = \mu RT \implies \alpha_V = \frac{1}{T}$$
Derivation for Ideal Gas $\alpha_V$:
At constant pressure $P$: $P\Delta V = \mu R \Delta T$
Divide by $PV = \mu RT \implies \frac{\Delta V}{V} = \frac{\Delta T}{T}$
$$\alpha_V = \frac{1}{V}\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta T} = \frac{1}{T} \quad (\text{At } 0^\circ\text{C}, \alpha_V \approx 3.67 \times 10^{-3}\text{ K}^{-1})$$

2. Thermal Expansion in Solids & Liquids MUST KNOW
Coefficients of Expansion ($\alpha_l, \alpha_a, \alpha_v$)
$$\Delta l = \alpha_l l \Delta T, \quad \Delta A = \alpha_a A \Delta T, \quad \Delta V = \alpha_v V \Delta T$$ $$\alpha_l : \alpha_a : \alpha_v = 1 : 2 : 3 \quad \implies \quad \alpha_a = 2\alpha_l, \quad \alpha_v = 3\alpha_l$$
SI Units: $\text{K}^{-1}$ or $(^\circ\text{C})^{-1}$
Thermal Stress in Fixed Rod: $$\text{Stress} = \frac{F}{A} = Y \left(\frac{\Delta l}{l}\right) = Y \alpha_l \Delta T$$ $$\text{Compressive Force: } F = A Y \alpha_l \Delta T$$
Density Variation with Temp: $$\rho' = \frac{\rho}{1 + \alpha_v \Delta T} \approx \rho(1 - \alpha_v \Delta T)$$ (Valid for $\alpha_v \Delta T \ll 1$)
Proof / Derivation: Relationship $\alpha_v = 3\alpha_l$ for Isotropic Solids
1 Consider a cube of initial length $l$. Expanded length $l' = l + \Delta l = l(1 + \alpha_l \Delta T)$.
2 New Volume $V' = (l')^3 = l^3 (1 + \alpha_l \Delta T)^3 = V (1 + 3\alpha_l \Delta T + 3\alpha_l^2 \Delta T^2 + \alpha_l^3 \Delta T^3)$.
3 Since $\alpha_l \sim 10^{-5}\text{ K}^{-1}$, terms containing $\alpha_l^2$ and $\alpha_l^3$ are negligible.
4 $\Delta V = V' - V \approx 3\alpha_l V \Delta T$. Comparing with $\Delta V = \alpha_v V \Delta T \implies \mathbf{\alpha_v = 3\alpha_l}$.
CAUTION: Anomalous Expansion of Water
Between $0^\circ\text{C}$ and $4^\circ\text{C}$, water contracts on heating ($\alpha_v$ is negative). Water has maximum density at $4^\circ\text{C}$ ($\rho_{\max} = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3$). This allows aquatic life to survive at the bottom of frozen lakes.
3. Calorimetry & Change of State MUST KNOW
Heat Capacity, Specific Heat & Latent Heat
$$\Delta Q = m s \Delta T, \quad \Delta Q = \mu C \Delta T, \quad \Delta Q = m L$$
Specific Heat Capacity ($s$): $$s = \frac{1}{m}\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta T} \quad [\text{SI: J kg}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}]$$ $s_{\text{water}} = 4186\text{ J kg}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} \approx 1\text{ cal g}^{-1}(^\circ\text{C})^{-1}$
Latent Heat ($L$): $$L = \frac{Q}{m} \quad [\text{SI: J kg}^{-1}]$$ $L_f \text{ (Ice)} = 3.33 \times 10^5\text{ J/kg } (80\text{ cal/g})$
$L_v \text{ (Steam)} = 22.6 \times 10^5\text{ J/kg } (540\text{ cal/g})$
Numerical Solving Workflow: Principle of Calorimetry
$$\text{Heat Lost by Hot Bodies} = \text{Heat Gained by Cold Bodies}$$
Identify Phases → Check Temp Bounds → Include $mL$ if Phase Changes → Equate $Q_{\text{lost}} = Q_{\text{gained}}$
Note: Always verify if available heat from hot body is sufficient to melt/vaporize the cold body completely.

4. Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection & Radiation EXAM FAVOURITE
Thermal Conduction (Fourier's Law) & Thermal Resistance
$$H = \frac{dQ}{dt} = K A \frac{T_C - T_D}{L} = \frac{\Delta T}{R_{th}} \quad \text{where} \quad R_{th} = \frac{L}{KA}$$
Combination Type Equivalent Thermal Resistance ($R_{th,eq}$) Equivalent Conductivity ($K_{eq}$)
Series Combination ($L = L_1 + L_2, A_1=A_2$) $R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 = \frac{L_1}{K_1 A} + \frac{L_2}{K_2 A}$ $K_{eq} = \frac{L_1 + L_2}{\frac{L_1}{K_1} + \frac{L_2}{K_2}} \quad \left[\text{If } L_1=L_2: K_{eq} = \frac{2K_1 K_2}{K_1 + K_2}\right]$
Parallel Combination ($A = A_1 + A_2, L_1=L_2$) $\frac{1}{R_{eq}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}$ $K_{eq} = \frac{K_1 A_1 + K_2 A_2}{A_1 + A_2} \quad \left[\text{If } A_1=A_2: K_{eq} = \frac{K_1 + K_2}{2}\right]$
Radiation Laws: Stefan-Boltzmann & Wien's Displacement Law
$$H = e \sigma A T^4, \quad H_{\text{net}} = e \sigma A (T^4 - T_s^4), \quad \lambda_m T = b$$
Stefan-Boltzmann Law:
$\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8}\text{ W m}^{-2}\text{K}^{-4}$ (Stefan's Constant)
$e = \text{Emissivity } (0 \le e \le 1); \quad e = 1 \text{ for Blackbody}$
Wien's Displacement Law:
$b = 2.9 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m K}$ (Wien's Constant)
$\lambda_m \propto \frac{1}{T} \implies \text{As } T \uparrow, \text{ Peak } \lambda_m \text{ shifts to shorter } \lambda$.
5. Newton's Law of Cooling SHORTCUT METHODS
Differential & Approximate Forms
$$\text{Exact: } \frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_s) \implies \ln\left(\frac{T_2 - T_s}{T_1 - T_s}\right) = -kt$$ $$\text{Approximate (For Numericals): } \frac{T_1 - T_2}{t} = K \left( \frac{T_1 + T_2}{2} - T_s \right)$$
Conditions for Validity:
  • 1. Temperature difference $(T - T_s)$ must be small ($\lesssim 30^\circ\text{C}$).
  • 2. Heat loss occurs predominantly via radiation & forced convection.
Linear Graph Verification:
Plot of $\ln(T - T_s)$ vs time $t$ gives a straight line with slope $=-k$.
Step-by-Step Solved Exam Numerical (Newton's Law of Cooling)
Problem: A body cools from $80^\circ\text{C}$ to $50^\circ\text{C}$ in $5\text{ minutes}$ in a room at $20^\circ\text{C}$. Calculate the time taken to cool from $60^\circ\text{C}$ to $30^\circ\text{C}$.
1 Case 1: $T_1 = 80^\circ\text{C}, T_2 = 50^\circ\text{C}, T_s = 20^\circ\text{C}, t = 5\text{ min}$.
Average $T_{\text{avg}} = \frac{80+50}{2} = 65^\circ\text{C}$.
Formula: $\frac{80 - 50}{5} = K(65 - 20) \implies \frac{30}{5} = K(45) \implies K = \frac{6}{45} = \frac{2}{9}\text{ min}^{-1}$.
2 Case 2: $T_1 = 60^\circ\text{C}, T_2 = 30^\circ\text{C}, T_s = 20^\circ\text{C}$.
Average $T_{\text{avg}}' = \frac{60+30}{2} = 45^\circ\text{C}$.
Formula: $\frac{60 - 30}{t'} = K(45 - 20) \implies \frac{30}{t'} = \left(\frac{2}{9}\right)(25)$.
3 Solve for $t'$: $t' = \frac{30 \times 9}{50} = \mathbf{5.4\text{ minutes}} = \mathbf{5\text{ min } 24\text{ s}}$.
